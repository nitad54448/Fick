Theory & Documentation

Onsager Multi-Component Diffusion Solver • v9.2

1. Overview

This tool simulates multi-component cation diffusion in oxide crystals using the Onsager transport formalism. Unlike simple Fickian diffusion where species diffuse independently, real crystals exhibit correlated motion. Every jump requires a vacancy, and vacancies are shared by all species, producing cross-coupling between fluxes.

2. Physical Models

Initially the deposited film contains 100% selected non-Mg cations, while the substrate is pure MgO. The domain spans film plus slab. Both external boundaries are sealed; Mg can diffuse into the film during annealing. Set time to zero to inspect the initial state. One finite-volume cell crossing the initial interface contains the volume-weighted average of both layers. Film percentages are cation-site fractions, not oxide mass or formula-unit fractions; this constant-site-density model does not resolve separate oxide phases, oxygen transport or density changes.

2.1 Onsager Flux Equation

For each species $i$, the flux is governed by the gradients of all concentrations, split into kinetic cross-coupling and thermodynamic driving forces:

$$ J_i = - \sum_{j} \sum_{k} D_{ik}^{kin} \Phi_{kj} \nabla C_j $$
  • $D_{ik}^{kin}$: Onsager-Manning kinetic coefficients (Vacancy Wind / Site sharing).
  • $\Phi_{kj}$: Thermodynamic factor matrix derived from the $\Omega$-matrix.

2.2 Kinetics: The Q-Matrix & Vacancies

The local tracer diffusivity depends on the concentration-weighted activation barrier ($Q$) and the dynamic vacancy fraction ($V_{factor}$):

$$ D_i^*(x) = D_{0,i}\, \exp\left(-\frac{\sum_j C_j(x)\, Q_{ij}}{k_B T}\right) \times V_{factor}(x) $$

Note: $V_{factor}$ is calculated dynamically. Aliovalent donors (Valence > 2) increase vacancies, accelerating diffusion. Acceptors (Valence < 2) suppress free vacancies down to the intrinsic Schottky limit.

2.3 Thermodynamics: The Ω-Matrix

All symmetric pair interactions, including Mg–dopant pairs, enter the regular-solution free energy. Index 0 denotes Mg; all cation fractions sum to one. The chemical-potential gradients drive the flux:

$$ g = k_B T\sum_i C_i\ln C_i + h, \qquad h=\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}C_iC_j $$ $$ F_i = \nabla C_i + \frac{C_i}{k_BT}\left(\sum_j\Omega_{ij}\nabla C_j-\nabla h\right) $$ $$ J_i = -D_i^*F_i + C_i\sum_k D_k^*F_k $$

For one dopant, the thermodynamic factor reduces to $1-2\Omega C(1-C)/(k_BT)$. Unstable or marginal compositions stop with a message; phase separation requires a gradient-energy model. The existing vacancy correction and parameter database remain phenomenological.

The finite-volume solver uses upwind drift for every cation, including Mg, with a common lattice velocity that enforces zero total cation flux. The time step follows current outgoing transport rates; failed trial steps are retried without clipping or renormalizing concentrations. There is no step-count cutoff. The main Run calculation button becomes Stop while the worker runs and immediately cancels it when clicked. Upwind transport adds numerical smoothing, so compare grid resolutions for sharp profiles. Reference: NIST FiPy manual, numerical schemes.

Reference: MIT lecture on spinodal decomposition and gradient energy.
